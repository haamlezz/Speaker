TMW-27 Floorstander

Follow this guide to build the cabinet: tweeter, 4-inch midrange and 8-inch woofer, with a separate sealed chamber for the midrange and a 46-litre vented chamber for the woofer. All dimensions are in millimetres. The drawings are to scale but not to a fixed print scale, so always work from the written numbers.

Before you cut

  • Buy the drivers first. Check their real cut-out diameter, flange size and depth against pages 2 and 3.
  • Enter the woofer's real datasheet values in the box simulator (web/index.html). If it gives a different volume or port length, change the woofer chamber height or the port length to match.
  • Build the second speaker as a mirror image: the tweeter offset goes toward the inner edge on both.
  • Cut a test piece to check your saw and router settings before cutting the baffles.
  • Wear eye and ear protection and use a dust mask. MDF and ply dust is harmful.
Airtight matters. A leak in the woofer chamber or the divider ruins the bass tuning. Seal every inside joint and every wire hole.

Baffle routing

  • Outer layer: cut-out hole, plus a recess the size of the flange Ø and as deep as the flange, so each driver sits flush.
  • Inner layer: cut-out hole only, then chamfer its back edge at 45° for the woofer and midrange so air can move freely behind the cone.
  • The tweeter only needs its cut-out through both layers.
  • After gluing the two layers, rout a 25 mm roundover on the two vertical front edges and the top front edge.
  • Mount the drivers with M5 T-nuts and bolts, not wood screws.
Driver spacing check: tweeter bottom edge to mid top edge ≈ 10 mm, mid bottom edge to woofer top edge ≈ 22 mm (with a 104 mm tweeter, 106 mm mid and 230 mm woofer flange). If your flanges are bigger, move the woofer down, not the tweeter.

Port

  • Ø76 mm inside, flared tube. Total length 349 mm, measured from the underside of the bottom panel.
  • Cut it 360 mm long and trim to tune (page 8).
  • Keep at least 76 mm of open space around its top end.
  • Round the hole on the underside of the bottom panel with about an 18 mm radius.

Damping

  • Woofer chamber: butyl or bitumen pads on the sides and back, then 25 mm felt on the sides, back and floor. Leave the middle open.
  • Mid chamber: fill it loosely with wool or polyester, about 3/4 full.

Window braces (×2)

  • Outer size 234 × 286, the same as the inside of the box.
  • Window 170 wide × 200 deep, leaving 32 mm side members and 43 mm front and back members.
  • Glue all four edges to the baffle, sides and back. The brace only stiffens the walls if it is glued on every side.
  • The port tube passes through the lower brace window. Its centre is 135 mm from either outer side and 170 mm behind the inner face of the baffle (206 mm from the front of the speaker).

Plinth and gap

  • Plinth 300 × 380 × 25 mm, overhanging the cabinet by 15 mm each side and 20 mm front and back.
  • Four hardwood spacers, 34 × 34 × 40 mm, inset 12 mm from the cabinet corners. Glue or screw them to the cabinet and screw the plinth on.
  • The 40 mm gap must stay open on all four sides, because the port breathes through it. Never stand the speaker on carpet without the plinth.
  • Fit spikes or rubber feet under the plinth.

Stage 3

Build steps

  1. 01Cut all panels (page 5). Label each one and mark which speaker it belongs to, left or right.
  2. 02Clamp the two baffle layers together. Drill pilot holes at the driver centres: 80, 195 and 385 mm from the top, with the tweeter 35 mm off-centre (mirrored on the second speaker).
  3. 03Rout the cut-outs through both layers with the circle jig. Rout the flange recesses in the outer layer only.
  4. 04Chamfer the back of the woofer and mid holes 45° in the inner layer. Fit the T-nuts from the back.
  5. 05Glue the two baffle layers together (spread glue thinly and evenly). Clamp flat and leave overnight.
  6. 06Cut the windows in the two braces and the port hole in the bottom panel. Round the underside of the port hole.
  7. 07Glue one side to the baffle, square it up, then glue in the top, divider, braces and bottom at the heights on page 3. Then the second side.
  8. 08Glue the port tube through the bottom panel and the lower brace window. Its top end should be 349 mm above the underside of the bottom panel, or longer for now.
  9. 09Drill the terminal holes in the back panel (woofer chamber, about 150 mm above the bottom) and a wire hole in the divider.
  10. 10Glue on the back. Once dry, run sealant along every inside joint you can reach through the driver holes, especially around the divider.
  11. 11Rout the 25 mm roundover on the front edges. Sand, then finish (veneer, oil or paint). Mask the driver recesses.
  12. 12Fit the damping pads and the 25 mm felt in the woofer chamber. Keep the area above the port clear.
  13. 13Run the wires: woofer, mid and tweeter pairs to the terminals. Seal the divider hole and the terminal holes with hot glue.
  14. 14Fill the mid chamber with wool. Put gasket tape on each driver flange and mount the drivers with bolts. Tighten evenly in a star pattern, finger-tight plus a quarter turn.
  15. 15Leak test: play a 20–30 Hz tone at low level and listen or feel around the joints, terminals and drivers. Air should only come out of the port.
  16. 16Fix the spacers and the plinth. Check that the 40 mm gap is open all round.
Stage 4

Tuning and setup

DSP channelFiltersStart gain
WooferHigh-pass 20 Hz LR 24 dB/oct (protection) · low-pass 350 Hz LR 24 dB/oct · low shelf +3 to +6 dB at ~425 Hz (baffle step)0 dB
MidrangeHigh-pass 350 Hz LR 24 dB/oct · low-pass 2600 Hz LR 24 dB/oct · same low shelf0 dB
TweeterHigh-pass 2600 Hz LR 24 dB/oct−5 dB
AllAll drivers in the same polarity. Measure the delays (tweeter usually 0.1–0.3 ms)—
  1. T1Check the port tuning before any other step. Measure the woofer's impedance. The dip between the two bass peaks should be at 27 Hz. If it is higher, the port is too short, so fit a longer tube. If it is lower, trim 5 mm at a time. Write the result here: Hz
  2. T2Check the polarity of each driver with a 1.5 V battery: the cone moves forward when + is on the red terminal.
  3. T3Put the mic 1 m away at tweeter height. Measure each driver alone and set the gains so all three bands are level.
  4. T4Use REW's impulse alignment to set the mid and tweeter delays. Check for a smooth sum through 350 Hz and 2600 Hz, with no dip.
  5. T5Set the baffle-step shelf: +6 dB far from walls, +3 to +4 dB if the speakers stand 30–60 cm from the back wall.
  6. T6At your listening seat, only cut room peaks below 300 Hz. Don't boost dips, and leave the treble alone.
  7. T7Place the speakers 50–80 cm from the back wall in an equilateral triangle with your seat, toed in toward you, tweeters near ear height (≈ 1025 mm with the plinth).
Never run the woofer without the 20 Hz protection filter. Below the port tuning, the cone is no longer held back by the air in the box, and the simulation shows it passing its 7.3 mm limit at 60 W.
Background

Why it is designed this way

Sealed boxes stop at 54 Hz. The vented 46 L box reaches 27 Hz without a hump.

Smaller ports blow air too fast and chuff. Ø76 mm is the largest that still fits in the box.

Wave simulation: rounded edges and an off-centre tweeter halve the edge-diffraction ripple.

The full active 3-way system after DSP correction, flat within about ±0.6 dB.

Based on Thiele (1961) and Small (1973) for vented boxes, Olson (1951) for cabinet edge diffraction, Harwood and Iverson for panel resonance, and Salvatti/Devantier/Button and Bezzola for port design. The simulation code is in the repository under simulator/. Driver values are nominal, so measure your finished speakers.
